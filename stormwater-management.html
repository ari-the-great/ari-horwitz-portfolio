Stormwater Management for Engineers Without Borders, Vanderbilt

A part of the EPA's Campus RainWorks challenge, this box is designed to be placed underground on Vanderbilt's Campus to better control excess water buildup in the city. The box contains flow, turbidity, PH, and conductivity sensors.

Mechanical Design for Sensor Components

One of my main roles was designing the mechanical system for the flow sensor. Using a potentiometer, connected to a three bar linkage system, when water rises in the flow box, we can measure this flow rate. We had to manually calibrate the correlation between potentiometer resistance and water level, and created a regression formula so it can run on its own. We also included a tube to siphon the water when it reaches a certain height in the box. 

Prototpying

This project involved 3D printing of almost every single component. Although 3D printing is not ideal for waterproofing, we went through many filaments and tolerances to best water seal the electrical components. Since it will be exposed to nature, the box also had to survive the extreme changes in temperature and remain function whether expanding or contracting.
